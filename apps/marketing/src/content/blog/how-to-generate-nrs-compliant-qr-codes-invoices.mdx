<p>Every NRS-compliant invoice must include a QR code. This code allows buyers, auditors, and the NRS to verify your invoice in real-time — confirming that it's legitimate, the TINs are valid, and the tax calculations are correct. Here's how to generate QR codes on your invoices for free.</p>

<h2>Why QR Codes Are Mandatory</h2>
<p>The NRS Merchant Buyer Solution (MBS) uses QR codes as the verification mechanism. When a buyer receives your invoice, they scan the QR code to:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Verify the invoice exists in the NRS system</li>
    <li>Confirm the seller's TIN is valid</li>
    <li>Check the VAT and WHT calculations</li>
    <li>Validate the invoice hasn't been tampered with</li>
</ul>
<p>Without a QR code, your invoice is essentially unverifiable — and non-compliant.</p>

<h2>What Information Is Encoded in the QR Code?</h2>
<p>A compliant invoice QR code contains:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Invoice number and date</li>
    <li>Seller's TIN and business name</li>
    <li>Buyer's TIN (for B2B)</li>
    <li>Total amount and currency</li>
    <li>VAT amount and WHT amount</li>
    <li>Digital signature hash</li>
    <li>Verification URL</li>
</ul>
<p>When scanned, this data is cross-referenced with the NRS database in real-time.</p>

<h2>Common QR Code Mistakes</h2>

<h3>Mistake 1: Using a Generic QR Generator</h3>
<p>Generic QR generators (like those that encode just a URL) don't include the NRS-required data fields. Your QR code must encode structured invoice data, not just a link.</p>

<h3>Mistake 2: Low Resolution</h3>
<p>QR codes must be at least 150x150 pixels to scan reliably. Lower resolutions fail on mobile devices and in printed invoices.</p>

<h3>Mistake 3: No Error Correction</h3>
<p>QR codes should use at least "M" (Medium) error correction level. This ensures the code remains scannable even if part of the invoice is damaged or obscured.</p>

<h3>Mistake 4: Missing Data Fields</h3>
<p>If your QR code doesn't include the TIN, VAT amount, or digital signature, it won't pass NRS verification. Every field matters.</p>

<h2>QR Code Best Practices for Nigerian Invoices</h2>
<ul>
    <li>Place the QR code in the bottom-right corner of the invoice</li>
    <li>Use a white background with high contrast</li>
    <li>Include a caption: "Scan to verify invoice"</li>
    <li>Test the QR code with at least 3 different phone cameras before sending</li>
    <li>Ensure the QR code is included in the PDF, not just the HTML version</li>
</ul>
