## The Arithmetic of 7.5% VAT

At its core, Value Added Tax is a consumption tax. It is crucial to internalize a fundamental operational principle: **VAT is not your money.** You are merely a collection agent for the government.

The current standard VAT rate in Nigeria is 7.5%.

### The Right Way vs. The Wrong Way

Let's say your base fee for a software development sprint is &#8358;1,000,000.

**The Amateur Approach (Inclusive VAT):**
You bill the client &#8358;1,000,000. When it's time to remit taxes, you realize the 7.5% comes out of your pocket. You effectively just gave the client a discount.
- Your Revenue: &#8358;930,232.56
- FIRS Cut: &#8358;69,767.44

**The Operator Approach (Exclusive VAT):**
You clearly communicate that your fee is exclusive of VAT. You add the 7.5% on top of the base fee.
- Base Fee: &#8358;1,000,000
- VAT (7.5%): &#8358;75,000
- Total Invoice Amount: &#8358;1,075,000

The client pays &#8358;1,075,000. You keep your &#8358;1,000,000. You remit the &#8358;75,000 to the FIRS.

## Vatable vs. Non-Vatable Items

Not everything you sell attracts VAT. The Nigerian government exempts specific items to protect basic living costs and essential services.

**Common Exemptions:**
- Basic food items (rice, garri, yam)
- Medical and pharmaceutical products
- Educational materials and books
- Baby products
- Exported services (If you are a Nigerian freelancer building software for a client in the US, you generally do not charge Nigerian VAT, provided the service is entirely consumed outside Nigeria).

*Strategic Insight:* Always separate vatable and non-vatable line items on your invoice. If you bundle them, corporate clients will likely reject the invoice or apply Withholding Tax (WHT) incorrectly, delaying your payment by weeks.

## The Corporate Client Reality: Why Presentation Matters

Corporate entities in Nigeria—banks, telcos, FMCGs—run on rigorous procurement and finance systems. If your invoice does not clearly separate the subtotal, the 7.5% VAT, and the gross total, their accounts payable system will flag it.

They need to see the exact VAT amount to claim their input VAT credits. An amateur invoice sent via a WhatsApp PDF without proper tax line items screams "high risk" to a corporate finance controller. It triggers back-and-forth emails, endless corrections, and ultimately, a frozen cash flow.

The FIRS is rolling out the Merchant Buyer Solution (MBS) e-invoicing mandate in 2026. This means invoices will eventually need to be validated in real-time with the FIRS system via APIs.

Businesses still running on manual Word documents will be locked out of the formal economy. Transitioning to a digital invoicing infrastructure now isn't just about looking professional; it's about future-proofing your business operations against regulatory shockwaves.

## Execution Checklist for Your Next Invoice

Before you send your next invoice, verify these three things:

1. **Explicit Line Item:** Is VAT clearly labeled as "VAT (7.5%)" directly under the subtotal?
2. **Correct Calculation:** Is the VAT exactly 7.5% of the *vatable* subtotal?
3. **TIN Inclusion:** Does your invoice prominently display your Tax Identification Number (TIN)? Corporate clients legally cannot process your VAT payment without it.
