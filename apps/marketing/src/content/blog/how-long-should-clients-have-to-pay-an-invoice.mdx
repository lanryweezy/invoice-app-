The biggest silent killer of Nigerian businesses isn't lack of sales. It is delayed payments.

You close a major deal. You deliver exceptional work. You send an invoice over WhatsApp or email. And then... silence. The client says, "I will sort you later this week," and "later" turns into three weeks of awkward follow-ups, constrained cashflow, and quiet resentment.

In the African SME landscape, capital velocity—how fast money moves into your business—is the ultimate growth metric. When you allow clients to arbitrarily dictate when they pay you, you aren't just delaying revenue; you are providing zero-interest loans to other businesses at the expense of your own operations.

So, how long should a client actually have to pay an invoice? And more importantly, how do you build a financial system that enforces your terms without destroying client relationships?

Here is the operational framework for structuring and enforcing payment terms that top agencies, operators, and consultants use to maintain healthy cashflow.

## The Myth of "Net 30" in the Nigerian Context

Globally, "Net 30" (payment due within 30 days of the invoice date) has been the default standard for B2B transactions. But in Nigeria, Net 30 often morphs into "Net Whenever."

When you issue a Net 30 invoice without strict enforcement mechanisms, you are implicitly telling the client that the urgency of payment is low.

**Here is the reality of payment terms:**
*   **Due on Receipt:** Best for small, transactional services, freelance projects, or one-off deliverables. It sets an expectation of immediate settlement.
*   **Net 7 / Net 15:** The sweet spot for modern service businesses, agencies, and ongoing consulting. It provides a reasonable window for the client's finance team to process payment while protecting your cashflow.
*   **Net 30 / Net 60:** Typically reserved for enterprise clients or government contracts where procurement processes are rigidly institutionalized. If you accept these terms, your pricing must account for the delayed capital velocity.

If you don't explicitly define your payment timeline, the client will default to their own convenience.

## The Psychology of Professional Invoicing

Why do some businesses get paid in 24 hours while others wait 45 days? It comes down to perceived institutional authority.

When you send a plain Word document or a WhatsApp message saying, "Please pay X amount to this account," you are signaling that your financial operations are informal. Informal systems invite casual payment behaviors.

**High-performing businesses use structural friction to enforce urgency:**

1.  **System-Generated Invoices:** An invoice sent through a platform like InvoiceApp.ng carries inherent authority. It looks like it came from an automated financial system, not a desperate founder.
2.  **Explicit Terms:** Your invoice must clearly state "Due Date: October 15" rather than just "Net 15." Ambiguity breeds delay.
3.  **Bank Account Permanence:** Constantly changing account details or sending personal account numbers erodes trust. Professional businesses use dedicated corporate accounts.

## Engineering Cashflow: How to Guarantee Faster Payments

Setting the terms is only half the battle. Enforcement requires a mix of business leverage and automated systems.

### 1. The Upfront Commitment Strategy
Never finance your client's projects. For project-based work, the standard should be 50% upfront, 50% upon completion. For high-risk or new clients, push for 70/30. This ensures your baseline operational costs are covered and filters out clients with structural liquidity problems.

### 2. Late Payment Penalties (The Deterrent)
Include a clear clause in your contract and on the invoice: *"A late fee of 5% applies to payments not received within 7 days of the due date."* You don't always have to enforce it, but its presence on the invoice acts as a powerful psychological deterrent against deprioritization.

### 3. Frictionless Settlement
The harder it is to pay you, the longer it will take. If a client has to log into their banking app, manually enter a 10-digit account number, and verify the name, they might "leave it for later."
Integrate payment gateways directly into your invoices. When an invoice includes a "Pay Now" button that routes through Paystack or Flutterwave via InvoiceApp.ng, you eliminate the operational friction of settlement.

### 4. Automated Follow-Ups
The mental toll of chasing payments is exhausting. It strains the client relationship and wastes high-leverage time. Remove the human element from follow-ups. Set up an automated sequence:
*   *Day -2:* Gentle reminder that an invoice is due soon.
*   *Day 0:* Invoice is due today.
*   *Day +3:* Automated late notice.

When the system sends the reminder, you preserve the relationship while maintaining strict financial boundaries.
